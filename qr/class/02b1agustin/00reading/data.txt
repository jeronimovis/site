# Part 3 – Part 2: finding the perfect match

## Listening for detail | Individual · 9 minutes
> Watch the second clip. Complete the sentences with the exact words you hear. Then read the transcript to check.

[play https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VrWO0QyaCX4 05:52 07:38 "Clip B – the perfect place for each person"]

[transcript "Transcript (after you have finished)"]
Teacher Phill: Remember that there are always three extra places which you don't need to use. Also, the place has to be perfect for the person, which means that the people have to be able to find everything they want in the place you match them to. The first thing you should do when you get to Reading Part 2 is to check what exactly the five different people want from their perfect place.
Teacher Phill: Read each description very carefully and underline the things that are important to each person. In our example from before, this could look like this. You can see in different colors the three things that are important to Jenny and Matt when visiting a city market.
Teacher Phill: It is key for you to find these three things for all five people in order to be able to complete the task, otherwise you will have to simply guess the correct answers, and we definitely don't want that to happen. After analyzing what exactly each of the five people want in their perfect city market, we can now look at the eight different texts and find the perfect option for each person.
Teacher Phill: Start with the first text and see if you can find anything that matches what any of the five people want. If you see something, check if the place offers all three things that the person wants, because remember, the place needs to be a perfect fit.
Teacher Phill: In this step, you need to be careful with synonyms and paraphrasing. Synonyms are words that have the same meaning, and paraphrasing means to say the same thing by using different words. For example, "He doesn't want to be your boyfriend anymore," and "He wants to break up with you." The two sentences have the same meaning, but I use different words to express the same idea.
[/transcript]

[teacher]
**Video:** Teacher Phill, “Cambridge B1 Preliminary (PET) Reading - Everything You Need to Know” (22:57). The intro (00:00–00:21) and outro (22:20–22:57) are skipped.

**Clips**
- Clip A 01:10–02:53 – the four papers, six reading parts, 25% of the grade; Part 1 (five short texts, choose A/B/C with the same meaning; options all look correct).
- Clip B 05:52–07:38 – Part 2 matching: three extra places, "perfect fit", underlining what each person wants, synonyms and paraphrasing (break up example).
- Clip C 15:30–17:24 – Part 5 multiple-choice cloze: the coconut tree example; eliminating "temperature" and "weather" (uncountable); "climate"; reading the text again.
- Clip D 20:26–22:20 – choosing the order of tasks, 45 minutes including the answer sheet, timing yourself.

**Timing:** 5 + 5 + 6 + 9 + 7 + 7 + 5 + 4 + 12 = 60 minutes. Play each clip twice if students need it (Clip B especially).

**On-screen material:** the video shows sample exam questions and coloured highlights. Show the video on the screen rather than audio only, so B1 learners can follow the examples.

**Grammar points**
- Modals for advice and obligation: "you don't have to go through the tasks from Part 1 to Part 6" (20:42), "The first thing you should do" (05:52), "three extra places which you don't need to use" (05:52), "it can't be correct" (04:01), "you need to be careful" (07:14), "you can only write one word" (19:30). Highlight the difference between *don't have to* (not necessary) and *mustn't* (not allowed).
- If-sentences for advice: "If you see something, check if…" (06:57), "if you feel that you get tired very quickly, it might be a good idea to…" (20:42), "if you know how it works, it is actually quite easy" (10:17), "if you find a mistake or two, it's already helpful" (20:01). Point out: present simple in the if-clause, no *will*.

**Key vocabulary:** underline, eliminate, synonym, paraphrase, perfect fit, answer sheet, extra, guess, break up with, uncountable noun, climate, time yourself, fresh.

**If time is short:** cut Clip C (Part 4 of the lesson) or reduce speaking to two questions.

**Fast finishers / homework:**
- Watch 08:46–12:04 (Part 3 and Question 15) and write three tips for Part 3 in their own words.
- Do a real Reading Part 2 task from a practice test, underlining the three things each person wants, and note the synonyms and paraphrases they found.
- Time themselves on a full reading paper and write their personal order of tasks.
[/teacher]
