1Warm-up

Pairs, then share8 minutes

  1. When did you last fly? Walk your partner through your routine from arriving at the airport to sitting on the plane.
  2. Carry-on only, or do you check a bag? Why?
  3. Has an airline ever lost or delayed your luggage? What happened?
  4. What do you think happens to your suitcase after you check in? Who — or what — moves it?
  5. Guess: Dubai Airport's morning rush lasts about 3 hours. How many bags do you think it processes in that time? Write your number down — you'll check it later.

Bonus: spot the mistake

In Part 1 the narrator calls DXB "a three-digit code". Is that the right word? What would you call it? And how close was your guess from the warm-up?

Answer key

Bonus: DXB is made of letters, not digits — it's a three-letter airport code (an IATA code). The morning rush figure is about 50,000 bags.

4Discussion

Small groups15 minutes

Thinking like an engineer

  1. The system doesn't track the bag — it tracks the tray. Why is that a smart design? Can you think of a similar idea in software (IDs, references, keys)?
  2. The narrator says the system "simply can't be allowed to fail". If you were in charge, how would you monitor it? What would trigger an alert?
  3. Green = normal, yellow = queuing, red = fault. Do your dashboards or status pages work like this? Is three colours enough?
  4. Bags that arrive early wait in storage until it's "their time". Where have you seen queues or scheduling like this in your work?
  5. "It's like the robots have taken over." Where should humans stay in the loop in a system like this — and where are they just in the way?

Thinking like a traveller

  1. Do you put a tracker (like an AirTag) in your luggage? Would you trust the airline's system or your own device more?
  2. Which airport has impressed you most — and which one was a nightmare?
  3. Where's the next place you want to travel? What piece of tech would make that trip easier?

Useful language

  • If I were designing it, I'd…
  • The bottleneck is probably…
  • What happens if… goes down?
  • It's a single point of failure.
  • I'd rather… than…
  • That reminds me of…
